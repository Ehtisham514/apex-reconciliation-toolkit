# Integrated three-statement management model

**Analyzing a bank?** [Open the bank model](https://ehtisham514.github.io/apex-reconciliation-toolkit/financial_intelligence/bank-lab/) or [review the filings, assumptions and verification results](bank-lab/README.md). To reproduce the bank run from the repository root, use `python financial_intelligence/integrated_model.py bank --output-dir bank-results`.

A five-year, single-entity planning model that links an income statement, balance sheet, indirect cash flow statement and debt schedule across base, downside and upside cases. The templates are blank; there are no sample companies, assumptions or transaction records.

## What it is designed to do

- Carry revenue, operating costs, working capital, capital spending, debt, cash and retained earnings through five forecast years.
- Show each scenario's input assumptions beside its outputs, with a source reference, source date, preparer, reviewer and review status.
- Check the opening actual balance sheet, forecast balance sheets, cash-flow cash roll-forward, term debt, revolver, net PP&E and retained earnings roll-forwards.
- Size revolver draws to maintain a user-entered minimum cash balance, up to the stated facility limit, and sweep excess cash to repay the opening revolver.
- Run with one reporting currency and a user-selected minor-unit precision. The model does not translate currencies or consolidate entities.

It is a management-planning model, not an accounting system, statutory statement preparer, credit decision engine, regulatory capital/liquidity model, audited valuation, or independently validated institutional model. I cannot confirm that JPMorgan Chase or any other bank would use it. A financial institution would need its own fit-for-purpose review, validation, data controls and approvals before any reliance.

## Run it

Use Python 3.11 or newer. The model uses the Python standard library.

From the repository root:

    python financial_intelligence/integrated_model.py --actuals financial_intelligence/integrated_model_actuals.template.json --assumptions financial_intelligence/integrated_model_assumptions.template.csv --output-dir output/integrated-model

The template files intentionally contain blank inputs, so the command stops until you enter and review actuals and assumptions. The script does not silently fill missing values.

It writes forecast.csv and checks.csv only after all inputs validate and linked-statement controls pass. Preserve an input copy and output folder for each model version; rerunning against the same output directory replaces those two output files.

To run only one case, add --scenario base, --scenario downside or --scenario upside. The default runs all three.

## Required actuals

Fill integrated_model_actuals.template.json with:
- Entity name, three-letter reporting currency code, currency minor-unit digits and base year.
- The closing base-year income statement amounts for revenue, cost of goods sold and operating expenses.
- Closing base-year balance sheet amounts for cash, receivables, inventory, net property and equipment, payables, term debt, revolver, share capital and retained earnings.
- Minimum cash and the total revolver facility limit.

The opening balance sheet must balance within one minor currency unit. The model stops if it does not; it will not create a balancing plug. The opening revolver must not exceed its limit.

Examples of minor-unit precision include 0 digits for a currency with no minor unit, 2 for a currency with two decimal places and 3 for a currency with three decimal places. Confirm the appropriate precision for the chosen currency before use. Amounts in the JSON are plain numbers without currency symbols or thousands separators.

## Required assumptions

Fill all 15 scenario/year rows in integrated_model_assumptions.template.csv: Base, Downside and Upside for forecast years 1 through 5. Values are explicit per year and per scenario; no case is generated by a hidden multiplier.

- Growth and tax / cost ratios are decimals: 0.05 means 5%.
- Working-capital days are days outstanding.
- Debt rates are annual decimal rates.
- Term principal repayments and dividends are currency amounts.
- Every row requires a source reference, ISO date, preparer, reviewer and status: DRAFT, REVIEWED or APPROVED.
- APPROVED requires a reviewer different from the preparer. Approval status is recorded; the software does not verify identity or authority.
- A source reference can be a private file name, management-approved budget or other controlled record. Do not put confidential source documents or personal data in a public repository.

## Model formulas and conventions

Income statement:
- Revenue = prior-year revenue × (1 + revenue growth).
- COGS, operating expenses, capex and depreciation use the period's explicit percentage assumptions.
- EBITDA = revenue − COGS − operating expenses.
- EBIT = EBITDA − depreciation.
- Interest = opening term debt × term-debt rate + opening revolver × revolver rate.
- Tax estimate = max(pretax income, 0) × tax rate. Loss carryforwards and jurisdiction-specific tax rules are not modeled.

Working capital:
- Receivables = revenue × AR days ÷ 365.
- Inventory = COGS × inventory days ÷ 365.
- Payables = COGS × AP days ÷ 365.
- Operating cash flow = net income + depreciation − increase in receivables − increase in inventory + increase in payables.

Cash and debt:
- Investing cash flow is negative capex.
- Term debt principal repayment and dividends are user inputs.
- If pre-revolver cash is below the minimum, the model draws the available revolver at period end up to the facility limit. If cash exceeds the minimum while an opening revolver balance exists, excess cash repays that opening balance.
- Interest is based on opening debt balances. Period-end draws do not accrue interest until the following forecast year. Term repayments, revolver draws and cash sweeps are treated as period-end events.
- Ending cash equals opening cash + operating cash flow + investing cash flow + financing cash flow. Liquidity shortfall is shown when available revolver capacity cannot restore the minimum cash balance.

Balance sheet:
- Net PP&E rolls forward as opening net PP&E + capex − depreciation.
- Retained earnings roll forward as opening retained earnings + net income − dividends.
- Share capital is held constant. No share issuance, buybacks, leases, goodwill, deferred taxes, accrued liabilities, provisions, acquisitions or other balance-sheet accounts are modeled.

All amounts are rounded half up to the selected currency's minor-unit precision. The cash-flow presentation uses operating, investing and financing sections as a management-planning layout. Map statement presentation and classifications to the entity's applicable accounting framework and policy. IFRS references: [IAS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-1-presentation-of-financial-statements/) and [IAS 7 Statement of Cash Flows](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-7-statement-of-cash-flows.html/). This model does not claim compliance with IFRS or any other reporting framework.

## Control outputs

checks.csv records the currency-level differences and the source/review metadata. Core controls must pass before output is written. Review the source references and approval status as well as the arithmetic checks. A zero balance-sheet difference only demonstrates that the modeled formulas tie; it does not prove the source data, assumptions, accounting treatment or forecast is correct.

Forecast rows also include each model driver, source date, preparer, reviewer and review status so a reviewer can trace the result back to the entered assumptions. A liquidity shortfall is a risk flag, not committed financing.

## Boundaries to understand

This version is intentionally a single-entity planning model. It has no FX translation, multi-entity consolidation, intercompany eliminations, monthly seasonality, revenue recognition schedule, debt covenant testing, interest circularity, cash tax timing, tax-loss carryforwards, deferred tax, leases, pension balances, purchase accounting, scenario probabilities, Monte Carlo simulation or external software connections. It is not designed for lending approval, regulatory reporting, investment advice or financial-statement issuance.
